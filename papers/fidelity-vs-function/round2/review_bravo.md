## Summary

Paper presents compression methods for co-trained draft heads in speculative-decoding models (Qwen3.5-9B, Qwen3.6-27B). Authors establish two-family taxonomy: fidelity cuts (quantization) versus function cuts (pruning, low-rank, distillation). Measured via per-token verification oracle. Fidelity cuts cost ≤1.9 acceptance points under domain shift; function cuts pay 10–19 points. Recipe: trim vocabulary to 32k hottest tokens + quantize to NVFP4 → 212MB artifact, 1.8–2.7x speedup. Pre-deployment audit: harm-rate metric (cost per disagreement) classifies family; disagreement rate ranks off-distribution tax (Spearman 1.0, p=0.0002). Mechanism: rare-content function accumulates negligible mass on finite calibration samples, so selection statistics miss exactly what shift demands.

## Major concerns

### 1. **Absence of independent replication data**

- **Issue:** Results rest on single proprietary engine with one third-party "independent" replication lacking detail.
- **Where:** §4.1, line mentioning "third configuration built independently in the engine's own lane."
- **Why it matters:** Core speedup claims (1.8–2.7x) and acceptance deltas derive from "bw24, a production CUDA engine" whose kernel implementations, batching logic, and memory-access patterns remain unspecified. Third "configuration" is vague (same engine? different team? different kernel?), with no commit hash, reproduction script, or separate artifact. A proprietary kernel could mask interaction effects (e.g., bandwidth saved by trim could be negated by cache-line misalignment in another engine). No standard framework verification (llama.cpp, vLLM, TGI) provided.
- **What would address it:** Publish complete reproduction package (model export script, engine commit hash or self-contained runtime, inference harness) and demonstrate 2+ independent engines (e.g., llama.cpp + vLLM) reproducing rank order of speedups. At minimum: provide kernel-level bandwidth measurements (PCIe Gen3/4/5 GB/s observed per arm) to decouple hardware effects from compression effects.

### 2. **Calibration-set composition and hot-vocabulary generalizability both opaque**

- **Issue:** Vocabulary ranking uses "text the model itself generated," with no corpus composition, length, prompt diversity, temperature, or seed disclosure. Hot set defined as top 32,768 + specials; no ablation of k ∈ [16k, 64k], no coverage curves stratified by domain. Table 1's "95 to 99% hot coverage" is a 4-point band—at 95%, one in twenty tokens falls outside the draft's reachable set; which domains pay that 5% is unreported.
- **Where:** §3.1 trim description; §4.0 "ranked hottest on the model's own generated text"; Table 1 note.
- **Why it matters:** If ranking corpus over-represents English tech docs, then Hebrew or PubMed prompts pay disproportionate OOV penalties (verifier must reject draft 5–20% more often purely from vocabulary mismatch, independent of compression quality). The paper claims trim is "shift-proof" yet provides no stratified coverage table (e.g., MT-Bench English 99% vs German Wiki 92%). Without stratification, "95 to 99%" conflates in-distribution floors with cross-domain ceilings, hiding exactly the shift-brittleness the paper diagnoses in function cuts. A 32k threshold is arbitrary; no sensitivity analysis to k appears.
- **What would address it:** Report ranking corpus composition (tokens, domains, temperature). Provide per-domain coverage table (at least: en, de, zh, code, bio). Ablate k ∈ [8k, 16k, 32k, 64k] on speedup–coverage Pareto frontier. Show acceptance–retention of trim alone (not trim+quant) stratified by coverage bins to isolate OOV tax from numeric noise.

### 3. **Domain-shift suite unrepresentative and underpowered**

- **Issue:** "Genuine domain shift" suite is 24 documents (German, Chinese, Hebrew, Russian Wiki, PubMed, C4) where reference head drops from 0.88 to 0.36 accuracy. Distribution unspecified (e.g., 4 docs per language? 10 PubMed + 14 C4?). 24-doc suite yields wide CIs ("§4.2, each function tax individually excludes zero at 95% confidence" suggests CI widths ~5–8 points). 0.36 reference accuracy on "shifted" set is catastrophically low (64% of drafts wrong)—either model is fundamentally unfit for that traffic, or suite is adversarially hard, limiting external validity.
- **Where:** §4.2 robustness section; §8.2 limitations acknowledge "24 documents."
- **Why it matters:** Central claim (function cuts pay 10–19 points under shift, fidelity ≤1.9) rests on this suite. If 24-doc sample over-represents pathological cases (e.g., code-switched text, OCR errors in Wiki scrapes), observed taxes overstate real deployment risk. Conversely, if sample under-represents model's actual serving distribution (e.g., all docs <2k tokens while Table 1 tests to 5.9k), then long-context shift is unmeasured. Reference dropping to 0.36 means 64% of positions are model failures independent of compression—can't disentangle "compression broke rare function" from "model never had function for this domain." No per-language or per-domain breakdown provided, so Hebrew tax (say) could be 30 points while German is 5 points, all averaged into "10–19."
- **What would address it:** Expand shift suite to ≥100 prompts per domain, balanced by prompt length bins matching Table 1 (short 256-token, long 512-token generations). Report per-domain retention table. Justify reference accuracy drop (0.88→0.36): is this realistic serving traffic or artificial stress test? If stress test, rename as "adversarial-shift suite" and add genuine-serving shift suite where reference ≥0.7.

### 4. **Audit validation circular on ranking metric**

- **Issue:** Audit's Spearman 1.0 (p=0.0002) from §5.3 ranks seven arms' *disagreement rates* on in-distribution data against their *retention losses* on the domain-shift suite. But disagreement rate is monotone in quantization bit-width and pruning ratio by construction (more compression → more disagreement), and retention loss is also monotone in those same parameters. The "exact ranking" could reflect parameter ordering rather than predictive power of harm-rate mechanism. No cross-validation: audit was designed after seeing the shift-suite results, then validated on the same seven arms that produced those results.
- **Where:** §5.3 validation; Spearman 1.0 claim.
- **Why it matters:** Audit's value is *predicting* unseen deployment harm from in-distribution signals. A post-hoc ranking of seven points on a scatterplot where x and y both correlate with the same latent variable (compression severity) is not predictive validation—it's a monotonicity check. No held-out arms (e.g., Q6 quantization, 40% pruning, rank-192 SVD) tested: would harm-rate still separate families and disagreement still rank severity if we hadn't cherry-picked these seven? No adversarial test: does harm-rate misclassify any cases (e.g., does a hypothetical structured quantization with high disagreement but low harm fool the metric)?
- **What would address it:** Pre-register audit on seven *training* arms, then test on ≥5 held-out arms (e.g., Q6, Q5, 40% prune, rank-384 LoRA, mixed block formats). Report per-arm audit scores and shift-suite taxes in table, with held-out results clearly marked. Alternatively: leave-one-out cross-validation where each arm is predicted from the other six's audit→tax regression. Show at least one failure mode (e.g., "structured quantization X breaks the harm-rate heuristic because…").

### 5. **Selection-scheme study tests interventions that don't isolate proposed mechanism**

- **Issue:** §4.4 and §6 claim rare-content channels accumulate negligible calibration mass, so *all* finite-sample statistics miss tail function. Six schemes tested: global mass, Wanda-style, wide calibration (100 docs), online EMA, hot-logit energy, divergence-conditioned (restore channels by activation at failure positions). All fail identically. But none directly tests the mechanism. If rare channels fire rarely, then an *oracle* scheme with access to ground-truth "fires on rare content" labels should succeed. Authors don't test this—no synthetic setup where channels are labeled by firing frequency quantiles, and pruning is conditioned on quantile membership. Without oracle test, can't distinguish "selection from finite samples is fundamentally ill-posed" from "our six statistics happen to be bad."
- **Where:** §4.4, §6.1–6.2 mechanism.
- **Why it matters:** Central theoretical claim (finite samples cannot see rare function) is falsifiable but not falsified here. If mechanism is correct, then (a) a statistic computed on *infinite* data (or a very large corpus, say 10M tokens) should close the gap, and (b) an oracle that directly labels rare-firing channels should recover pruning performance. Authors test neither. The 100-doc "wide calibration" is still tiny (likely <200k tokens); no ablation to 1M, 10M tokens. Divergence-conditioned scheme is clever but indirect—restoring channels by activation at *pruned head's* failures tests selection under already-broken function, not selection under full function. This confounds the test.
- **What would address it:** (A) Ablate calibration size: 10k, 100k, 1M, 10M tokens. If mechanism correct, gap should persist at all sizes (tail scales with sample). (B) Oracle baseline: cluster channels by firing frequency on *shift-suite data* (not calibration), label bottom-10% quantile as "rare," prune from top 90% only. If rare-channel hypothesis holds, oracle pruning should pay near-zero shift tax. (C) Alternatively, synthetic setup: construct head where channels 1–100 serve common bigrams, channels 101–200 serve rare language X. Prune using English-only calibration. Measure whether English-trained statistics systematically spare or kill the language-X channels. This would directly test claim.

### 6. **Megachain measurements and claim retraction under-explained**

- **Issue:** §4.0 mentions "one earlier headline of ours (a 2.68x sampled cell) failed this protocol; we retracted it in the lab record." No details on what "lab record" means (internal document? public preprint?), what the failed protocol was (likely single-prompt measurement from before 3-seed aggregation), or whether other reported cells are similarly vulnerable. Abstract's "All negative results appear in full" suggests transparency, yet this retraction is a footnote. If single-prompt variance is ±12 points (sd 13 over 20 seeds), then a 2.68x cell with 1-seed could be 2.2x with another seed—a 20% swing. Which Table 1 cells have sd >10 acceptance points across seeds? Not reported.
- **Where:** §4.0 setup; "megachain tail" phrase in git-log snippet.
- **Why it matters:** Sampled-decoding results throughout Table 1 rest on 3-seed aggregation. If within-cell variance is sd=13 and between-cell differences are <5 points, then seeds dominate signal. Authors correctly aggregate to stabilize, but don't report per-cell variances—can't assess whether trim-Q8 vs trim-NVFP4's 0.2-point gap is real or seed noise. Retracted headline suggests prior results were reported prematurely; how many other claims were revised? Reproducibility requires knowing which cells are fragile.
- **What would address it:** (A) Report per-cell standard deviation across seeds in Table 1 (add sd column). (B) Expand retraction: in supplement, show the original 2.68x cell's seed-by-seed breakdown and explain what changed in methodology. (C) Show at least two example single-prompt trajectories with 20-seed runs (as in abstract's "20 seeds on one long prompt span 57 points") to illustrate the noise that aggregation controls. This demonstrates protocol's necessity rather than asserting it.

### 7. **Cross-base-quantization result (§4.2) tests only one base quantization level**

- **Issue:** Authors test head compressions against bf16 base and NVFP4-quantized base, claiming "head-compression costs stay base-independent" (4.5 vs 6.0 acceptance-point drop for 4-bit head). But base is quantized to same format (NVFP4) as head—no cross-format test (e.g., head=NVFP4, base=INT4 or AWQ). If base and head quantization errors correlate (both introduce rounding at similar scales), observed independence could be an artifact of format matching. Also tests only one base quantization level (NVFP4); no ablation to Q8 base or Q6 base to check dose-response.
- **Where:** §4.2, paragraph on quantized-base cross.
- **Why it matters:** Claim is "fidelity cuts compound in speed rather than damage." If true, deployment can quantize base and head independently without multiplicative accuracy loss—important for memory-constrained serving. But evidence is one format (NVFP4) at one level (4-bit). If base is quantized to INT4 or GPTQ and head to NVFP4, do they still not interact? Different formats have different outlier-preservation properties; NVFP4's denormals may cover for head errors that INT4 wouldn't. Need at least 2x2 grid: {Q8, Q4} x {Q8, Q4} for base x head, showing retention stays additive, not multiplicative.
- **What would address it:** Expand to 3x3 grid: base ∈ {bf16, Q8, NVFP4}, head ∈ {bf16, Q8, NVFP4}. Report acceptance and retention for all nine combinations on at least one domain-shift corpus. Show that head-compression tax (measured as acceptance drop from bf16-head row) is constant across base columns, and vice versa. If interaction exists, report it—would bound recipe's applicability.

## Minor concerns

- **§2.2** "Each position yields exact, binary, task-grounded measurement" — verification labels drafts as accepted/rejected, but a rejected draft can be *almost* correct (next-token probability 0.49 vs winner's 0.51). Calling this "exact" overstates: it's a label, not a quality score. Suggest "binary per-token label" rather than "exact measurement."
- **§3.1** "trim's ceiling is hot coverage: 95 to 99%" — this is floor, not ceiling (coverage cannot exceed 99%, but acceptance can drop below coverage if drafts are wrong even on hot tokens). Terminology flip.
- **§4.2** "Family gap of 14.5 retention points is significant at permutation-test floor for seven arms (p = 0.029)" — seven-arm permutation has 7! = 5040 possible rank assignments; if testing two-group split (4 vs 3), that's binom(7,3) = 35 splits. Floor p=1/35≈0.029 is not a test of effect size, just rank extremity. With seven arms, any gap is significant at p<0.05 by construction. Suggest bootstrapping retention-point gap to get CI on effect size, not just rank test.
- **§6.2** "Every channel spreads influence across whole vocabulary in near-uniform proportion (0.116 to 0.159)" — 0.116 to 0.159 is a 37% relative range; "narrow band" understates this. If hot rows are 13.2% of vocabulary (32k/248k), then 0.116 is 12% below uniform and 0.159 is 20% above—some channels *do* specialize. Suggest reporting this as relative deviation from uniform (±20%) rather than "near-uniform."
- **§7** Related work cites FR-Spec, QSpec, ML-SpecQD by name but no Hooker citation for "Pruning Identified Exemplars" (foundational tail-damage work) — only paraphrased. Add [arXiv:XXXX] or venue.
- **§8.1** Playbook row 2: "Stop. Pruning buys at most 90MB more" — 90MB is 7% of 1.33GB; where does this number come from? No 30% or 40% pruning result in Table 1 hits 90MB. Suggest showing calculation or citing the arm.
- **Table 1** columns "Short greedy" and "Long greedy" speedups are baselines (1.00x), but table doesn't define what "plain decode" uses (K=0? different engine mode?). Clarify in caption.
- **Figure 2** caption missing — figure shows cost breakdown but no label in text. Add caption.
- **Numerous "exact" claims** — Abstract: "outputs distribution-exact," §5.3: "exact ranking," §6.1: "exact positions." Reserve "exact" for mathematical guarantees (distribution-exact is correct; ranking/positions are empirical). Suggests overstatement pattern.

## Verdict

**Major revision**

Paper makes three valuable contributions (zero-training recipe, fidelity/function taxonomy, harm-rate audit) but seven major experimental-design flaws block acceptance. Most critical: (1) single proprietary engine with no standard-framework replication, (2) 24-document shift suite too small to support central claim, (3) audit validated post-hoc on same seven arms used to design it, (4) selection-mechanism study lacks oracle/infinite-data tests, (5) retracted result under-explained, raising reproducibility concerns. Secondary issues (calibration opacity, cross-base-quantization under-tested) further weaken generalizability. With expanded shift suite (≥100 prompts per domain), held-out arms for audit, oracle selection test, and reproduction in ≥2 standard frameworks, paper's claims would be credible. As written, results are suggestive but underpowered, and speedup claims are engine-specific until proven otherwise. Taxonomy is conceptually clean and mechanism plausible, but evidence doesn't yet meet the "exactly what compression destroys" standard the title promises.
