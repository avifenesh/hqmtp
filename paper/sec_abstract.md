# Abstract

Co-trained multi-token-prediction heads give Qwen- and DeepSeek-lineage models built-in speculative decoding at a fixed price: the head's full-vocabulary projection reads 1.33GB of weights per drafted token on a 9B model, and separate-artifact deployments hold 1 to 2GB of GPU memory. We compress the head and measure exactly what each kind of compression destroys, using the verifier itself as a per-token oracle. Every drafted token is a labeled test of whether compression changed the function. The measurements sort all compression into two families. Fidelity cuts (uniform precision reduction) cost at most 1.9 acceptance-retention points under severe domain shift, with 8-bit exactly free. Function cuts (channel pruning, low-rank factorization, distillation) keep 97% of their acceptance on calibration-register text and pay 10 to 19 retention points on genuinely shifted text. The tax grows with shift distance and survives six selection schemes, including selection calibrated on the pruned head's own observed failures. The mechanism is statistical: parameters serving rare content accumulate no mass on any finite calibration sample. Three practical results follow. A zero-training recipe (trim the vocabulary to the model's own 32k hottest tokens, quantize to NVFP4, verify full-vocabulary) cuts per-token reads to 0.21GB. It delivers 1.8 to 2.7x end-to-end speedup over plain decoding on Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.6-27B in a production CUDA engine, with outputs distribution-exact by construction. A forward-pass audit classifies any compressed draft before deployment: harm-per-disagreement separates the families (0.14 versus 0.28 to 0.54), and in-distribution disagreement ranks the off-distribution tax exactly on our hardest suite (Spearman 1.0, exact p = 0.0002). A measurement protocol makes sampled-decoding comparisons reproducible after we found single-prompt sampled cells swing 12 acceptance points across seeds. All negative results appear in full.
