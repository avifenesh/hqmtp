# 7 Related work

<!-- Positioning sentence: prior work established that quantized drafts run fast and that pruning forgets the tail; this paper compresses the co-trained head itself, unconfounds why one is safe and the other is never safe, and turns the answer into a pre-deployment instrument. -->

**Speculative decoding with cheap drafts.** FR-Spec trims the draft vocabulary at load time and reports 1 to 3% end-to-end gain in llama.cpp, and follow-up work [2603.05210] casts draft vocabulary size as a coverage-versus-latency optimization for separate draft models. On a bandwidth-optimized engine we measure trim alone at 1.8 to 2.7x, and it composes with quantization. QSpec [2410.11305] and ML-SpecQD [2503.13565] draft with quantized variants of the target model itself: QSpec switches quantization schemes within one model, ML-SpecQD direct-casts the target to MXFP4 as a plug-and-play draft. Both established that 4-bit drafting preserves acceptance; neither compresses a co-trained head (their drafts are whole-model copies measured in gigabytes), and neither analyzes which compressions are safe. A hierarchical-framework study [2505.22179] evaluates speculative decoding on quantized targets and finds the combination fragile in the memory dimension. EAGLE-family drafters and SlimSpec's low-rank drafter head address models without co-trained heads; the co-trained head's acceptance is the reason we compress it instead of replacing it.

**Compression damages the tail.** Hooker et al. established with Pruning Identified Exemplars that pruned vision classifiers sacrifice underrepresented examples first, with quantization milder. LLM-era evaluations (LLM-KICK [2310.01382] and successors) report broad capability loss from pruning that perplexity hides. This line measures example-level task accuracy on whole models with one cut type at a time. We add four things: the token-level oracle (millions of exact labels per GPU-hour), the 2x2 design separating dimensionality from calibration-dependence, the six-scheme selection study that turns an observation into a selection-theoretic account, and the dose-response measurement across three shift distances. The taxonomy's practical half, the harm-rate audit, has no counterpart in this line: prior work diagnoses damage post hoc on curated test sets, where the audit predicts it pre-deployment from in-distribution forward passes.

**Quantization robustness.** The QAT and post-training-quantization literature long ago established that uniform quantization degrades gracefully; we claim no priority there. Our contribution is comparative and mechanistic: at matched in-distribution retention, quantization's damage stays flat under distribution shift while every selective cut's damage grows, and the difference traces to what each cut removes.
